The **provinces of Thailand** are administrative divisions of the [government of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_government_of_Thailand "Organization of the government of Thailand").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Thailand#cite_note-act1991-4) The country is divided into 76 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province "Province") ( [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language "Thai language"): [จังหวัด](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%B8%88%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%94 "wikt:จังหวัด"), [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription "Royal Thai General System of Transcription"):_changwat_, pronounced[\[tɕāŋ.wàt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Thai "Help:IPA/Thai")) proper, with one additional special administrative area (the capital, Bangkok).[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Thailand#cite_note-DOPA-20170421-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Thailand#cite_note-6) They are the primary local government units and act as [juristic persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juridical_person "Juridical person"). They are divided into [amphoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Thailand "Districts of Thailand") (districts) which are further divided into [tambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambon "Tambon") (sub districts), the next lower level of local government.

All provinces form part of the partially devolved central government, or the regional government (ราชการส่วนภูมิภาค _ratchakan suan phumiphak_). Majority of public services, including police, prison, transport, public relation and others are still overseen and managed by the province on behalf of the central government. In 1938–1996, the Royal Thai Government proposed that each province should have a council, elected from people resided within that province. The council acts as an advisory and auditing body to the governor (ผู้ว่าราชการจังหวัด _phu wa ratchakan changwat_), who is appointed by the central government. In 1997, each province has its own [provincial administrative organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_administrative_organization "Provincial administrative organization") (องค์การบริหารส่วนจังหวัด _ongkan borihan suan changwat_), presided over by the president. The PAO manages some public services related to the province. It was expected that the PAO president will become the elected governor (instead of a centrally-appointed one), but the full devolution of the government has not happened. The PAO as well as other [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban "Thesaban") form part of the local self-governing government (ราชการส่วนท้องถิ่น _ratchakan suan thongthin_).

Bangkok, the sole special administrative area, combines the tasks of the provinces with that of a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality "Municipality"), including having an elected governor. The average area of the 76 provinces of Thailand plus Bangkok is about 6,663.89km2 (2,572.94sqmi), while its average population of all 77 divisions of Thailand is about 908,064 people.

Thailand's national government organisation is divided into three types: central government ( [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_government_ministries_of_Thailand "List of government ministries of Thailand"), bureaus and departments), provincial government (provinces and [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphoe "Amphoe")) and local government (Bangkok, [Pattaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattaya "Pattaya"), provincial administrative organisations, etc.).

A province, as part of the provincial government, is administered by a governor (ผู้ว่าราชการจังหวัด) who is appointed by the Minister of Interior. Bangkok, as part of the local government, is administered by a corporation called [Bangkok Metropolitan Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Metropolitan_Administration "Bangkok Metropolitan Administration"). The corporation is led by the Governor of Bangkok (ผู้ว่าราชการกรุงเทพมหานคร) who is directly elected by the citizens of Bangkok.

The provinces are named after their original main city, which may not necessarily still be the most populous city within the province today. Also, in several provinces the administration has been moved into a new building outside the city.

## History

### Before 1892

Many provinces date back to semi-independent local chiefdoms or kingdoms, which made up the [Ayutthaya Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom "Ayutthaya Kingdom"). The provinces were created around a capital city ( _[mueang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mueang "Mueang")_), and included surrounding villages or satellite towns. The provinces were administered either by a governor, who was appointed by the king or by a local ruling family, who were descendants of the old kings and princes of that area and had been given this privilege by the central king. De facto the king did not have much choice but to choose someone from the local nobility or an economically strong man, as against these local power groups the administration would have become impossible. The governor was not paid by the king, but instead financed himself and his administration by imposing local taxes himself. Every province was required to send an annual tribute to Bangkok.

The provinces were divided into four different classes. The first-class were the border provinces. The second-class were those that once had their own princely house. Third-class were provinces that were created by splitting them from other provinces. Fourth-class were provinces near the capital. Additionally tributary states like the principalities of [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na "Lan Na"), the [Laotian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos") kingdoms of [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane "Vientiane") and [Luang Prabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luang_Prabang "Luang Prabang"), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"), or the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_(ethnic_group) "Malays (ethnic group)") sultanate [Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah "Kedah") were also part of the country, but with more autonomy than the provinces. In this [Mandala system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandala_(Southeast_Asian_political_model) "Mandala (Southeast Asian political model)") the semi-independent countries sometimes were tributary to more than one country.

New provinces were created when the population of an area outgrew the administration, but also for political reasons. If a governor became too dominant in a region former satellite cities were elevated to provincial status, as was the case with [Maha Sarakham province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Sarakham_province "Maha Sarakham province").

Reforms of the provincial administration started in the 1870s under increased pressure from the [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism") states of the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). Agents were sent, especially to border areas, to impose more control on the provinces or tributary states.

### Administrative reform of 1892

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Map_of_Siam_in_1900.png/500px-Map_of_Siam_in_1900.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_Siam_in_1900.png) Map of Siam in 1900

At the end of the 19th century King [Chulalongkorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chulalongkorn "Chulalongkorn") reformed the central government. In 1892 the ministry, which previously had many overlapping responsibilities, was reorganized with clear missions as in Western administrations. Prince [Damrong Rajanubhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damrong_Rajanubhab "Damrong Rajanubhab") became minister of the Ministry of the North ( _[Mahatthai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatthai "Mahatthai")_), originally responsible for the northern administration. When the Ministry of the South ( _[Kalahom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahom "Kalahom")_) was dissolved in 1894, Prince Damrong became Minister of the Interior, responsible for the provincial administration of the whole country.

Starting in 1893 the already existing commissionaireships in some parts of the country were renamed "superintendent commissioner" ( _khaluang Thesaphiban_), and their area of responsibility was called a _[monthon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monthon "Monthon")_. In strategically important areas the monthon were created first, while in other areas the provinces kept their independence a bit longer. Several smaller provinces were reduced in status to an _amphoe_ (district) or even lower to a _tambon_ (sub-district) and included in a neighboring province, sometimes for administrative reasons, but sometimes to remove an uncooperative governor.

In some regions rebellions broke out against the new administrative system, usually induced by the local nobility fearing their loss of power. The most notable was the [Holy Man Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Man_Rebellion "Holy Man Rebellion") in 1902 in [Isan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan "Isan"). It was initially a messianic doomsday sect, but it also attacked government representatives in the northeast. The provincial town [Khemarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphoe_Khemarat "Amphoe Khemarat") was even burned by the rebels. After a few months the rebellion was beaten back.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Thailand#cite_note-9)

After 1916, the word _changwat_ became common to use for the provinces, partly to distinguish them from the provincial capital city ( _mueang_ or _amphoe mueang_), but also to stress the new administrative structure of the provinces.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Thailand#cite_note-10)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Siam_in_1900%2C_During_Rama_V%27s_Reign.jpg/500px-Siam_in_1900%2C_During_Rama_V%27s_Reign.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Siam_in_1900,_During_Rama_V's_Reign.jpg) Cities and Monthons in 1900[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Thailand#cite_note-11)

When Prince Damrong resigned in 1915, the whole country was divided into 19 monthon (including the area around Bangkok, which was under the responsibility of another ministry until 1922), with 72 provinces.

In December 1915 King [Vajiravudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajiravudh "Vajiravudh") announced the creation of regions ( _phak_), each administered by a viceroy ( _upparat_), to cover several monthon. Until 1922 four regions were established; however, in 1925 they were dissolved again. At the same time several monthon were merged, in an attempt to streamline administration and reduce costs.

### Since 1932

The monthons were dissolved when Thailand transformed from an absolute monarchy into a constitutional monarchy in 1932, making the provinces the top level administrative division again. Several smaller provinces were also abolished at that time. During World War II, several provinces around Bangkok were merged. These changes were undone after the war. Also the occupied area from French Indochina was organized into four provinces: [Phra Tabong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phra_Tabong_province "Phra Tabong province"), [Phibunsongkhram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phibunsongkhram_province "Phibunsongkhram province"), [Nakhon Champasak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhon_Champassak_province "Nakhon Champassak province") and [Lan Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Chang_province "Lan Chang province"). The current province of Sukhothai was at first known as Sawankhalok. It was renamed Sukhothai in 1939 (which is why the railway system goes to Sawankhalok city and not Sukhothai city). The province, Kalasin, was reestablished in 1947 after having been dissolved in 1932.

In 1972 Phra Nakhon and Thonburi provinces were merged to form the special administrative area of Bangkok, which combines the tasks of the provinces with that of a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban "Thesaban"), including having an elected governor.

Starting in the second half of the 20th century some provinces were newly created by splitting them off from bigger provinces. In 1975, Yasothon province was split off from Ubon Ratchathani. In 1977, Phayao province was created from districts formerly part of Chiang Rai. In 1982, Mukdahan was split off from Nakhon Phanom. In 1993 three provinces were created: [Sa Kaeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa_Kaeo_province "Sa Kaeo province") (split from Prachinburi), [Nong Bua Lamphu province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nong_Bua_Lamphu_province "Nong Bua Lamphu province") (split from Udon Thani), and [Amnat Charoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnat_Charoen_province "Amnat Charoen province") (split from Ubon Ratchathani). The newest province is [Bueng Kan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bueng_Kan_province "Bueng Kan province"), which was split off from Nong Khai effective 23 March 2011.
